A practice
of five
disciplines.

Each athlete receives one partner and one team. The partner who signs you negotiates your contract, drafts your brand strategy, and remains your point of contact a decade later. We do not pass clients down a desk.

The Approach

Most agencies are organised around volume. Ours is organised around the career. The same partner is present at the rookie contract, the apex deal, and the press conference that announces a retirement — and at every conversation between.

The five disciplines below are not departments. They are how a single, well-considered career is built.

§ I — Contract Negotiation

Worth, written
down on paper.

Forty-one negotiations completed last cycle. Our model: every offer is benchmarked against the next two markets, not the current one. Bonuses, guarantees, image-rights structures, exit and release clauses.

We treat contract structure as architecture. Years one through three secure the floor; years four onward earn the ceiling. Exit clauses are priced — not avoided.

  • Playing contracts All major leagues
  • Transfer files Cross-border, multi-party
  • Image-rights vehicles UK, ES, IT, DE
  • Release-clause counsel In-house + outside
§ II — Brand & Endorsements

A portfolio,
not a feed.

Three to five enduring partnerships rather than thirty short ones. We build briefs, vet creative, and protect the likeness for the back half of a career.

An athlete's likeness is an estate. The job is to keep it valuable for fifty years, not loud for five.

“Decline gracefully. Sign rarely. Renew indefinitely.”
§ III — Media & Voice

The voice is
the asset.

Production, documentary, long-form interviews, ghost-edited columns. Run by a former editor of The Athletic Quarterly.

i.

Editorial standards

Every interview placement reviewed against the same criteria a magazine commissioning editor would apply.

ii.

Documentary & long-form

Three features in active production with two streaming studios. Athlete as executive producer where appropriate.

iii.

Press counsel

Pre-tournament briefings. Crisis communications. The discipline to say no when there is nothing to say.

§ V — Post-Career Architecture

Begin the
second act early.

Quietly, this is our best work. Board seats, broadcast contracts, founder roles, ownership stakes. We start this work five years before the final whistle, not the morning after.

Twelve of our retired clients now sit on the boards of the brands that once endorsed them. Four hold ownership stakes in the leagues they played in. One owns the broadcaster that televised her final.

— Coverage from rookie deal to retirement plus twenty.
