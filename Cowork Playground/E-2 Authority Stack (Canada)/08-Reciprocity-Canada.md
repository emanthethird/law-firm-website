# E-2 Authority Stack - Canada Reciprocity

## DOS Reciprocity Schedule - Canada

Source URL: https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/Visa-Reciprocity-and-Civil-Documents-by-Country/Canada.html

The Department of State publishes a country-by-country Reciprocity Schedule that sets the visa issuance (reciprocity) fee, number of entries, and validity period for each nonimmigrant visa class. The relevant figures for E classifications, as reproduced from the Canada page, are:

| Visa Classification | Fee  | Number of Entries | Validity Period |
|---------------------|------|-------------------|-----------------|
| E-1 (footnote 2)    | None | Multiple          | 60 Months       |
| E-2 (footnote 2)    | None | Multiple          | 60 Months       |
| E-2C (footnote 12)  | (blank in DOS table) | Multiple | 24 Months   |

DOS does not list a separate row for E-2S on the Canada reciprocity schedule. The E-2 row governs dependents (spouses and children) of E-2 principals on the Canada schedule, with the same fee/entries/validity as the principal (None / Multiple / 60 Months); the DOS table footnotes treat dependents under the same numbered footnote as the principal where applicable.

The DOS introduction to the schedule states:

- Fee means the reciprocity fee (visa issuance fee), which is in addition to the nonimmigrant visa application fee (MRV fee).
- Number of Entries means how many times the visa holder may seek entry to the United States. "Multiple" means without numerical limit during the validity period.
- Validity Period means the period from issuance during which the visa may be used for travel.

## Why This Matters - The Canada Reciprocity Advantage

Canada's reciprocity terms for E-1 and E-2 are among the most favorable in the world. The combination is:

- 60-month (5-year) validity period for both E-1 and E-2.
- Multiple entries.
- No reciprocity (issuance) fee.

For comparison, many treaty countries cap E-2 validity at 12, 24, or 36 months with a one-time or low-multiple entry count and a non-trivial reciprocity fee. The 60-month / multiple / no-fee triad means a Canadian E-2 principal can in practice complete one consular adjudication and then maintain status (subject to admission decisions, continuing E qualification, and any 22 CFR 41.122 revocation grounds) for five full years, with each U.S. admission generally yielding a new two-year E period of authorized stay under INA 214 and 8 CFR 214.2(e).

Operational consequences for Canadian E-2 counsel:

- Renewal cycles are tied to the visa expiration date (5-year cycle) rather than to shorter visa validity windows seen for other treaty countries.
- The no-fee posture reduces direct out-of-pocket cost at issuance, leaving the MRV fee (currently $315 for E-1/E-2, separate from reciprocity and not addressed in the reciprocity schedule) as the principal direct visa fee.
- The Multiple-entry posture allows business travel without separate entry-specific visas.
- For E-2C (CNMI long-term investor), the schedule provides 24-month validity, Multiple entries, and a fee column shown as blank on the DOS table; this is a separately administered classification and is rarely operative in a Canadian-applicant context but is preserved on the schedule for completeness.

## Historical Note

Canada's E-1 and E-2 entries on the DOS Treaty Country list show the same January 1, 1994 effective date (NAFTA) discussed in 06-Treaty-Canada. The 60-month / Multiple / No-fee reciprocity terms are the longstanding settings on the Canada schedule and have not been narrowed during the NAFTA-to-USMCA transition. Counsel reviewing pre-2020 case files will find the same reciprocity figures applied.

## Cross-Reference to 9 FAM 601

Source URL (general): https://fam.state.gov/fam/09FAM/09FAM060101.html

9 FAM 601.1 sets the State Department's policy framework for visa fees and reciprocity. The principle is that U.S. nonimmigrant visa validity and fees mirror, as nearly as practicable, the terms a foreign government applies to U.S. citizens applying for comparable visa categories abroad. The Canada-favorable 60-month / Multiple / No-fee posture for E visas reflects a reciprocal arrangement maintained at the diplomatic level over decades.

The reciprocity schedule operates as the authoritative published implementation of 9 FAM 601 for each country. Counsel arguing visa validity or fee questions should cite both the FAM 601 framework and the published Canada Reciprocity Schedule (the URL above).

Note: The full 9 FAM 601 text and the FAM treatment of reciprocity are catalogued in the FAM file (03-Foreign-Affairs-Manual) within this E-2 Authority Stack.
