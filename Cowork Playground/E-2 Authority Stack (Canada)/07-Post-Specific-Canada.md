# E-2 Authority Stack - Canadian Consular Posts

## Mission Canada E-Visa Architecture (Cross-Post Overview)

Source URL: https://ca.usembassy.gov/treaty-trader-and-investor-visas/
Source URL: https://ca.usembassy.gov/treaty-trader-and-investor-visas-faqs/

Mission Canada operates a centralized E-visa intake system. All E-visa cases across Canada use a single point of contact: EVisaCanada@state.gov. Cases are sorted into two streams:

1. New Cases and Renewals (first-time E applicants from a treaty enterprise and applicants whose treaty enterprise's registration is expired or expiring). These cases are reviewed exclusively by the U.S. Consulate General in Toronto. The applicant or company representative emails the complete supporting package to EVisaCanada@state.gov; the Toronto E-visa unit reviews the documents and only after document review is complete will instruct the applicant on appointment scheduling. The applicant should not schedule an appointment before receiving review-complete instructions.

2. Employees of Registered Companies and Dependents of Valid E-Visa Holders. Employees of currently registered E-visa companies and qualifying family members of current E-visa holders may schedule an appointment at Calgary, Montreal, Ottawa, Vancouver, or Toronto without first emailing documents. Self-identification of interview-waiver eligibility for renewing employees of the same registered company is permitted by emailing the supporting documents to EVisaCanada@state.gov with the subject line "IW Self-Identification for [Applicant Surname] with [Company Name]".

Company registration is valid for 5 years. If the registration is set to expire before the applicant's interview, the next applicant from the company must follow the New Cases and Renewals procedure (review at Toronto).

Effective September 6, 2025 (Mission Canada FAQ): All applicants, including those residing in the United States, are advised to submit visa applications in their country of residence or nationality, and must be able to demonstrate residence in the country where they apply if place of application is based on residency.

Effective May 1, 2025 (Mission Canada policy): NIV applicants interviewing at any consular section in Canada must present a DS-160 confirmation number that matches the application number used to schedule the appointment. Non-matching applications result in reschedule and potential new visa fees.

Non-Canada-resident applicants are advised on the Mission Canada visas page that wait times for appointments may be approximately 600 calendar days. This is the published Mission Canada wait-time notice and is most relevant for E applicants resident outside Canada who attempt to apply at a Canadian post.

Submission format (uniform across Mission Canada for New Cases and Renewals):

- PDF only, single attachment.
- Maximum 50 pages (excluding DS-160, DS-156E, G-28, appointment confirmation, civil documents, copy of passport bio-page, and tabs/dividers, which do not count toward the limit).
- Maximum 20 MB.
- No zip files. No online shared folders.
- Submissions failing format requirements are returned to the sender and may result in interview postponement.
- The applicant must bring a physical copy of the full package to the interview.

Processing time after interview: normally 7-10 business days for return of passport with visa via Canada Post Courier; not guaranteed.

Standard administrative-processing window (per Mission Canada FAQ): most administrative processing resolved within 60 days of the interview, but timing varies.

Expedited appointments: Mission Canada FAQ states business need alone is not a basis for expedited E-visa processing; expedited appointments are generally reserved for acute humanitarian needs. There is no premium processing fee option for E visas.

## U.S. Consulate General Toronto

Source URL: https://ca.usembassy.gov/u-s-consulate-toronto/
Source URL: https://ca.usembassy.gov/treaty-trader-and-investor-visas/
Source URL: https://ca.usembassy.gov/supporting-documentation-for-new-cases/

Adjudicates E visas: Yes - principal E-adjudicating post in Canada. Toronto is the exclusive post for all New Cases and Renewals (initial company registration and re-registration). Toronto also handles Employees of Registered Companies and Dependents.

Address: 360 University Avenue, Toronto. Visa Services entrance: 225 Simcoe Street.

Main line: 1-416-595-1700
Visa Information and Call Center: 1-437-887-1448
E-visa intake email: EVisaCanada@state.gov
Lost/stolen/damaged visa replacement (Toronto-issued): EvisaToronto@state.gov

Consul General: Baxter Hunt (in role since October 2023, per the consulate's own page).

Scheduling protocol for New Cases / Renewals:

1. Compile the full supporting documentation package per the Toronto checklist (Document Checklist below).
2. Email the single PDF (50 pages, 20 MB) to EVisaCanada@state.gov.
3. Wait for the Toronto E-visa unit to confirm document review is complete and to issue scheduling instructions.
4. Schedule the appointment as instructed; appear in person at 225 Simcoe Street.

Document Checklist for New Cases (verbatim from Toronto/Mission Canada page):

Tabbed sections with dividers; if more than two documents under a numbered tab, subdivide into lettered subsections with tabs.

Cover items (apply to all New Cases):

- DS-160 confirmation page for all applicants and dependents.
- Form DS-156E signed by an officer legally empowered to act in the company's name.
- 2x2 passport photo if not uploaded to DS-160.
- Cover letter summarizing the E-visa requirements and how the applicant(s) fulfill them. State exactly and precisely how the enterprise and applicant qualify. Do not include lengthy FAM or CFR citations.
- Comprehensive Index/Table of Contents.
- For each dependent: civil documents (government-issued birth/marriage certificates, adoption papers; originals required at interview; notarized copies, religious documents, hospital records, and short-form birth certificates that do not show parentage are not acceptable).

Tabbed evidentiary sections:

I. E-1 and E-2 Proof of Nationality
- Photocopy of passport biographical page.
- Evidence of legal status in Canada.

II. E-1 and E-2 Ownership Documents (A, B, or C):
- A. Sole Proprietorship: Business registration documents.
- B. Partnership: Partnership or joint venture agreement; share/stock certificates indicating total shares issued and outstanding.
- C. Corporation: Share/stock certificates indicating distribution of ownership (shares held by each firm and by individual owners); corporate matrix; if publicly traded on the principal stock exchange of a treaty country, a sample of recently published stock quotations.

III. E-1 Trade:
- Purchase orders; bills of lading; sales contracts/contracts for services; letters of credit; carrier inventories; trade brochures; insurance papers documenting commodities imported into the United States; accounts receivable and payable ledgers; client lists; other documents showing trade is substantial and that at least 51% is between the United States and the treaty country and that trade supports an ongoing business entity in the treaty country.

IV. E-2 Investment:
- A. Existing Enterprise (show purchase price): Tax valuation; market appraisal.
- B. New Enterprise (show estimated start-up cost): Trade Association Statistics; Chamber of Commerce Estimates; Market Surveys.
- C. Source of Investment: Personal statement of net worth prepared by a certified accountant; transactions showing payment of sold property or business (proof of property ownership and promissory notes) and rental income (lease agreements); voided investment certificates or internal bank vouchers and appropriate bank statement crediting proceeds; debit and credit advices for personal and/or business account withdrawals; audited financial statement; annual report of parent company; net worth statements from certified professional accountant.
- D. Evidence of Investment:
  - 1. Existing Enterprise: Escrow documents; signed purchase/sales agreement; closing and settlement papers; loan or mortgage documents; promissory notes; financial reports; tax returns; security agreements; assumption of lease agreement; business account statement for routine operations.
  - 2. New Enterprise: Inventory listing, shipment invoices of inventory, equipment, or business related property; receipts for inventory purchases; canceled checks or official payment receipts for expenditures; canceled check for first month's rent or full annual advance rent payment; lease agreement; purchase orders; improvement expenses; initial business accounts statements; wire transfer receipts.

V. E-2 Marginality:
- 1. Existing Business: U.S. corporate tax returns; latest audited financial statement or non-review statements; annual reports; payroll register; W-2 and W-4 tax forms; canceled checks for salaries paid and/or corresponding payroll account.
- 2. New Business: Payroll register, records of salaries paid (if any), employee data including names, rates of pay, copies of W-2s; financial projections for next 5 years, supported by a complete and thorough business plan; business income and current corporate tax returns; proof of registration, ownership, audited financial and review engagements.

VI. E-2 Real & Operating Commercial Enterprise:
- Occupational license; business license/permits; sales tax receipt; utility/telephone bills; business transaction records; current/commercial account statements; letters of credit; invoices from suppliers; advertising leaflets; business brochures/promotional literature; newspaper clippings.

VII. E-1 and E-2 Executive/Managerial/Supervisory/Essential Skills:
- Letter from the E-visa enterprise providing specific information on the applicant and reasons for U.S. assignment; the letter should explain the employee's role (title and duties), executive or supervisory responsibilities or specialist role, required education and knowledge level, employment experience, promotion progression or high-level training or special qualifications, and reasons why a U.S. citizen or LPR cannot fill the position (if the position is not managerial or supervisory).
- Letter from responsible official at the U.S. company or office identifying the need for the assigned employee.
- Organizational chart showing current U.S. staffing pattern.
- Evidence of executive, supervisory, or specialized knowledge, education, experience, skills, or training (certificates, diplomas, transcripts).

E-1 Renewals (Toronto): Same documentation as New Cases.

E-2 Renewals (Toronto), abbreviated package:
- MRV receipt.
- Form DS-160.
- Form DS-156E.
- Confirmation that the previous visa expired within the last 12 months.
- First two pages of the last two years of U.S. federal corporate tax returns.
- Schedule K of corporate tax return outlining ownership percentage, or independent auditor's report of financial statements.
- Most recent IRS Form W-2 for all employees and/or IRS Form 941 for the past year.

Timeline notes: Toronto historically carries the largest E-visa caseload in Mission Canada. The Mission Canada visas page notes that non-Canada residents can anticipate wait times of approximately 600 calendar days for an appointment. For Canadian-resident first-time E applicants, the document-review-first model means the timeline is driven by Toronto E-visa unit review turnaround rather than by appointment availability per se; in practice review takes weeks to months depending on volume.

## U.S. Consulate General Montreal

Source URL: https://ca.usembassy.gov/u-s-montreal-consulate/
Source URL: https://ca.usembassy.gov/treaty-trader-and-investor-visas/

Adjudicates E visas: Yes - for Employees of Registered Companies and Dependents of Valid E-Visa Holders only. New Cases and Renewals (initial company registration / re-registration) are not handled at Montreal; those are routed to Toronto regardless of where the applicant resides.

Intake: All Mission Canada E-visa correspondence uses EVisaCanada@state.gov; Montreal does not maintain a separate E-visa intake address for routine cases.

Submission format: Per Mission Canada-wide policy, employees of registered companies need not email supporting documents in advance and bring the full package to the interview (see Employees-of-Registered-Companies checklist below). For dependents applying separately, the documents are brought on the interview day, not pre-submitted.

Employees of Registered Companies - documents brought to interview:

- Appointment confirmation.
- Copy of Notice of E-Visa Company Registration.
- DS-160 confirmation page for all applicants and dependents.
- Updated DS-156E signed by an officer legally empowered to act in the company's name.
- 2x2 photo if not uploaded to DS-160.
- Letter from the company on the applicant and the U.S. assignment, plus the applicant's resume or CV.
- Signed statement from owner, CEO, corporate secretary, or executive attesting that there have been no substantial changes in the ownership structure or operations of the treaty enterprise that would jeopardize its E-visa qualifications since last registration. If changes, explain and document them.
- For dependents: civil documents (originals required at interview).
- Appropriate reciprocity fee, if required (none for Canada - see 08-Reciprocity-Canada).

Dependents applying separately (any post in Canada including Montreal):

- Appointment confirmation.
- DS-160 confirmation page.
- Proof of legal relationship (originals required: birth/marriage certificates, adoption papers; notarized copies, religious documents, hospital records, and short-form birth certificates that do not show parentage are not sufficient).
- 2x2 photo if not uploaded to DS-160.
- Photocopy of the principal applicant's E-visa.
- Appropriate reciprocity fee, if required.

Note: Dependents may not process visas until the principal applicant's E visa has been issued; the dependent must present a copy of the principal's visa at interview.

Personal-appearance rule: All applicants 14 to 80 years old must appear for a personal interview (Mission Canada-wide rule).

## U.S. Consulate General Vancouver

Source URL: https://ca.usembassy.gov/u-s-consulate-vancouver/
Source URL: https://ca.usembassy.gov/treaty-trader-and-investor-visas/

Adjudicates E visas: Yes - for Employees of Registered Companies and Dependents of Valid E-Visa Holders. Historically Vancouver also processed some E cases independently and the Mission Canada FAQ specifically references the Toronto and Vancouver E-visa email addresses (EvisaToronto@state.gov, EvisaVancouver@state.gov) for replacing lost, stolen, or damaged E visas previously issued at those posts.

Under the current centralized intake regime, the Mission Canada FAQ states that New Cases must apply at the U.S. Consulate General in Toronto. Practitioners should confirm Vancouver's current scope before routing a New Case there; the published Mission Canada policy directs all New Cases and Renewals (initial registration / re-registration) to Toronto via EVisaCanada@state.gov.

Submission protocol and documentation: Same Mission Canada-wide rules. For lost/stolen/damaged E visa previously issued in Vancouver, email EvisaVancouver@state.gov with a statement of circumstances; police report required if stolen.

## U.S. Consulate Quebec City

Source URL: https://ca.usembassy.gov/u-s-quebec-city-consulate/
Source URL: https://ca.usembassy.gov/treaty-trader-and-investor-visas/

Adjudicates E visas: No - Quebec City is not listed by Mission Canada as a post at which employees of registered E-visa companies or dependents may schedule an E-visa appointment. The Mission Canada FAQ specifies Calgary, Montreal, Ottawa, Vancouver, or Toronto as the posts where employees of currently registered E-visa companies and qualifying family members of current E-visa holders may schedule the next available appointment.

Quebec City handles American Citizen Services and limited consular functions but routes E applicants to Montreal (within Quebec) or to another Mission Canada E-adjudicating post.
