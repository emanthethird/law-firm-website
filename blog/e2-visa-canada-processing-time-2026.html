How Long Is the E-2 Visa Taking for Canadians Right Now?

By Emmanuel Okharedia • June 2026 • 8 min read

This is one of the most common questions I get from Canadian founders and owners who are thinking about moving their business to the United States. They have usually already decided the E-2 is the right path. What they want to know is the part nobody can fully promise: how long is this actually going to take if I start now?

So here is a current snapshot, written in mid-2026, of what Canadian applicants are realistically looking at today. I will give you the ranges we are seeing, walk through where the time actually goes, and point out the spots where 2026 looks a little different from previous years. One caveat up front: consular wait times move week to week, and anything I quote here is a current observation, not a guarantee. Always confirm against the live appointment data for the post you plan to use before you build a hard deadline around it.

The short version: Most Canadian applicants who start today should plan on roughly four to seven months from first engaging an attorney to actually entering the U.S. on E-2 status. The legal and business preparation work is the larger and more predictable part of that. The consular appointment is the part that swings with demand.

One Thing That Trips Up a Lot of Canadians First

Canadian citizens are visa-exempt for most visits to the United States. You are used to simply showing up at the border with your passport. That does not apply to the E-2. The E treaty visas are a specific exception: even as a Canadian, you have to apply for and be issued an E-2 visa through a U.S. consulate before you can enter in that status.

In practice this means your case goes through the same consular process as applicants from any other treaty country. For Canadians, E-2 cases are handled by the U.S. Consulate General in Toronto, which manages E visa adjudication for Canada. So when people ask me "how long is the E-2 taking right now," what they are really asking about is two things stacked together: how long the preparation takes, and how long the Toronto E unit is taking to schedule and decide cases at the moment.

What We Are Seeing in 2026, Phase by Phase

Phase 1
Business Setup and Investment
4 to 8 weeks

Forming the U.S. entity, opening the business bank account, moving funds across the border, signing a lease or closing on an acquisition, and getting the investment genuinely committed and at risk. For Canadians this phase is often smoother than average because moving funds from a Canadian bank and documenting their source tends to be cleaner than from many other countries. If you are buying an existing business, the closing timeline drives this phase.

Phase 2
Building the Application Package
4 to 6 weeks

The business plan, financial projections, source of funds evidence, entity documents, and the full set of supporting exhibits. This is the most time-intensive part and it is almost entirely within your control. The pace here depends mostly on how quickly you can hand over documents. This phase often overlaps with Phase 1 rather than following it cleanly.

Phase 3
Filing and Waiting for a Toronto Appointment
A few weeks to a couple of months

You submit the DS-160, pay the fees, and send the E visa package to the Toronto consulate according to its current instructions. As of mid-2026, the appointment wait for E-2 cases in Toronto has generally been more manageable than the long waits you see for visitor visas, but it does move with demand and seasonal volume. This is the single phase most likely to surprise you, in either direction, so it is the one to check live before you commit to dates.

Phase 4
Interview and Decision
Often at the interview, sometimes 1 to 3 weeks

The interview is usually short. The officer has already reviewed your file and is testing whether the business and your role match what is on paper. Most E-2 decisions come at the interview or within a few days. If the officer issues a 221(g) request for more information or administrative processing, the timeline extends while that review happens.

Phase 5
Passport Return and U.S. Entry
Roughly 1 to 2 weeks after approval

Your passport comes back with the E-2 visa foil, and you can enter the U.S. Canadians are often issued a five-year E-2 visa under the reciprocity schedule, and CBP typically admits you for a two-year period at the port of entry. The admission period and the visa validity are two separate clocks, so it is worth understanding both.

Why "Right Now" Matters: What's Different in 2026

The reason people ask about current timing rather than the textbook timeline is that the moving parts genuinely shift year to year. A few things worth knowing about the present moment:

The preparation phases are stable

Setting up the business and building the package takes about as long as it always has. These are not affected by consular volume. If your timeline slips, it is almost never because of these phases, it is because documents came in slowly or a business acquisition took longer to close.

Toronto appointment availability is the live variable

This is the number that actually changes. It tends to tighten in the busier travel seasons and loosen at quieter times. Because Toronto runs a dedicated E visa process, E-2 cases have generally not been stuck behind the much longer visitor visa queues, but you should never assume. Check the current appointment situation for your case before you promise a landlord, a school, or a seller a specific date.

Administrative processing is the wildcard

A small share of cases get pulled into additional review after the interview. There is no reliable way to predict or speed this up. The best protection is a clean, well-documented file that gives the officer no open questions to chase, which is exactly what good preparation is for.

Planning tip for Canadians with a hard date. If you are aiming to be operating in the U.S. by a specific time, say a fall school start for your kids or a lease that begins on a fixed day, count backward and give yourself at least six months. Seven is more comfortable. The most common reason a Canadian case feels rushed is not the consulate. It is starting the business setup and document gathering later than planned.

Can You Go Faster?

Sometimes, yes, but the leverage is almost all in the preparation, not the government's clock. The applicants who move fastest are the ones who get their documents in quickly, have a clean and well-documented source of funds, and do not need to chase down missing paperwork late in the process. Canadians often have a built-in advantage here because financial records and source of funds are usually straightforward to assemble.

What you generally cannot speed up is the consular side. There is no premium processing for a consular E-2 visa the way there is for some USCIS petitions. If you are already lawfully in the U.S. in another status, a change of status with USCIS is a different route with its own timeline, but most Canadian applicants are applying from Canada and going through Toronto.

The Bottom Line

For a Canadian starting today in mid-2026, four to seven months from first attorney conversation to U.S. entry is a realistic plan. The preparation work is the bulk of it and it is largely in your hands. The Toronto appointment is the part that floats, and as of now it has generally been workable, but it is the one number to verify live before you build a deadline around it.

If you have a date you are trying to hit, the single most useful thing you can do is start early and confirm the current consular wait for your case. We are happy to map a realistic timeline against your specific target.
