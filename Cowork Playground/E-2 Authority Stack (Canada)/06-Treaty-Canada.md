# E-2 Authority Stack - Canada Treaty Authority

## Overview: Canada's Basis for E-2 Eligibility

Canada is not a party to a traditional bilateral Treaty of Friendship, Commerce and Navigation (FCN) with the United States. Canadian nationals' access to E-1 (Treaty Trader) and E-2 (Treaty Investor) nonimmigrant classification derives from the North American Free Trade Agreement (NAFTA), effective January 1, 1994, and was continued without substantive change for E-1/E-2 purposes under the United States-Mexico-Canada Agreement (USMCA), which entered into force July 1, 2020.

NAFTA Chapter 16 (Temporary Entry for Business Persons) was the original framework; USMCA Chapter 16 (Temporary Entry for Business Persons), Section B (Traders and Investors) is the current governing chapter. The U.S. State Department's published Treaty Country List confirms Canada's E-1 and E-2 status with an effective date of January 1, 1994, which is the original NAFTA effective date carried over.

For E visa purposes the "treaty" relationship between the United States and Canada is therefore a multilateral free-trade-agreement-based relationship, not a bilateral FCN or BIT. Practical consequence: the principal E criteria (nationality of the enterprise, substantial trade/investment, real and operating enterprise, supervisory or essential-skills role for employees) are applied under INA 101(a)(15)(E), 22 CFR 41.51, and 9 FAM 402.9 in the same manner as for any other treaty country, with USMCA Chapter 16 Section B supplying the underlying treaty obligation.

## USMCA Chapter 16 - Temporary Entry for Business Persons

Source URL (USTR landing page): https://ustr.gov/trade-agreements/free-trade-agreements/united-states-mexico-canada-agreement/agreement-between

Source URL (Canada-hosted text, used to compile this section because the USTR PDF was not directly retrievable): https://www.international.gc.ca/trade-commerce/trade-agreements-accords-commerciaux/agr-acc/cusma-aceum/text-texte/16.aspx?lang=eng

Chapter 16 governs four categories of temporary entry: Business Visitors (Section A, corresponds to B-1), Traders and Investors (Section B, corresponds to E-1/E-2), Intra-Company Transferees (Section C, corresponds to L-1), and Professionals (Section D, corresponds to TN). For Canadian E-1/E-2 applicants, Section B is the operative provision.

### Article 16.1 - Definitions

"business person" means a citizen of a Party who is engaged in trade in goods, the supply of services or the conduct of investment activities.

"temporary entry" means entry into the territory of a Party by a business person of another Party without the intent to establish permanent residence.

### Article 16.2 - Scope

1. This Chapter applies to measures affecting the temporary entry of business persons of a Party into the territory of another Party.

2. This Chapter does not apply to measures affecting natural persons seeking access to the employment market of another Party, nor does it apply to measures regarding citizenship, nationality, residence or employment on a permanent basis.

3. Nothing in this Agreement prevents a Party from applying measures to regulate the entry of natural persons of another Party into, or their temporary stay in, its territory, including those measures necessary to protect the integrity of, and to ensure the orderly movement of natural persons across, its borders, provided that those measures are not applied in a manner as to nullify or impair the benefits accruing to any Party under this Chapter.

### Article 16.3 - General Obligations

1. Each Party shall apply its measures relating to this Chapter expeditiously so as to avoid unduly impairing or delaying trade in goods or services or the conduct of investment activities under this Agreement.

2. The Parties shall endeavor to develop and adopt common criteria, definitions and interpretations for the implementation of this Chapter.

### Article 16.4 - Grant of Temporary Entry

1. Each Party shall grant temporary entry to a business person who is otherwise qualified for entry under its measures relating to public health and safety and national security, in accordance with this Chapter, including Annex 16-A.

2. A Party may refuse to grant temporary entry or issue an immigration document authorizing employment to a business person where the temporary entry of that person might adversely affect:

(a) the settlement of a labor dispute that is in progress at the place or intended place of employment; or

(b) the employment of a person who is involved in that dispute.

3. If a Party refuses pursuant to paragraph 2, it shall provide written notice to the business person and promptly to the other Party of the reasons for the refusal.

4. Each Party shall limit any fees for processing applications for temporary entry of business persons to the approximate cost of services rendered.

5. The sole fact that a Party grants temporary entry to a business person of another Party pursuant to this Chapter does not exempt that business person from meeting any applicable licensing or other requirements, including any mandatory codes of conduct, to practice a profession or otherwise engage in business activities.

### Article 16.5 - Provision of Information

Each Party shall publish online or otherwise make publicly available explanatory material regarding the requirements for temporary entry under this Chapter so that a business person of another Party can become acquainted with them, and shall collect and maintain data on the granting of temporary entry under this Chapter.

### Article 16.6 - Temporary Entry Working Group

A trilateral Working Group meets at least annually to consider implementation, further facilitation measures, waiving labor certification tests for spouses of business persons admitted for more than one year under Sections B, C or D, proposed modifications, and other issues of common interest including processing technology.

### Article 16.7 - Dispute Settlement

A Party may not initiate dispute proceedings regarding a refusal to grant temporary entry or a particular case under Article 16.3(1) unless the matter involves a pattern of practice and the business person has exhausted available administrative remedies. Administrative remedies are deemed exhausted if a final determination has not been issued within one year of the institution of an administrative proceeding (where the delay is not attributable to the business person).

### Article 16.8 - Relation to Other Chapters

Except for Chapter 16 and a limited list of cross-cutting chapters (Initial Provisions and General Definitions, Administrative and Institutional Provisions, Dispute Settlement, Final Provisions, Publication, and Administrative Proceedings), the Agreement does not impose an obligation on a Party regarding its immigration measures.

### Annex 16-A, Section B - Traders and Investors (the E-1/E-2 provision)

1. Each Party shall grant temporary entry and provide confirming documentation to a business person seeking to:

(a) carry on substantial trade in goods or services principally between the territory of the Party of which the business person is a citizen and the territory of the Party into which entry is sought; or

(b) establish, develop, administer or provide advice or key technical services to the operation of an investment to which the business person or the business person's enterprise has committed, or is in the process of committing, a substantial amount of capital,

in a capacity that is supervisory, executive or involves essential skills, provided that the business person otherwise complies with the Party's measures applicable to temporary entry.

2. No Party shall:

(a) as a condition for temporary entry under paragraph 1, require labor certification tests or other procedures of similar effect; or

(b) impose or maintain a numerical restriction relating to temporary entry under paragraph 1.

3. Notwithstanding paragraph 2, a Party may require a business person seeking temporary entry under this Section to obtain a visa or its equivalent prior to entry. Before imposing a visa requirement, the Party shall consult with a Party whose business persons would be affected with a view to avoiding the imposition of the requirement. With respect to an existing visa requirement, a Party shall consult, on request, with a Party whose business persons are subject to the requirement with a view to its removal.

Practical drafting notes for Canadian E-1/E-2 applicants:

- The treaty text uses the same operative language familiar from the E classification: "substantial trade principally between" the two Parties (E-1); "establish, develop, administer or provide advice or key technical services to the operation of an investment" and "substantial amount of capital" (E-2); and the supervisory/executive/essential-skills capacity requirement (E-1 and E-2 employees).
- The phrase "substantial amount of capital" is not defined in USMCA; the operative U.S. interpretation is the proportionality test under 22 CFR 41.51(b)(8) and 9 FAM 402.9-6(D), not USMCA itself.
- The labor-certification-test prohibition in paragraph 2(a) is the treaty basis for the rule that E adjudication does not involve LCA or DOL prevailing wage tests.
- The visa requirement in paragraph 3 is what allows the United States to continue requiring Canadian E-1/E-2 applicants to apply for an E visa at a consular post (unlike Canadians applying for TN or B-1, which can be requested at the port of entry).

## NAFTA Legacy - Chapter 16 (the Predecessor Framework)

NAFTA Chapter 16 (Temporary Entry for Business Persons), effective January 1, 1994, was the original treaty basis for Canadian E-1/E-2 eligibility. The structure was substantively identical to the current USMCA Chapter 16: four sections corresponding to Business Visitors, Traders and Investors, Intra-Company Transferees, and Professionals, with parallel labor-dispute carve-outs and dispute-settlement limitations.

USMCA replaced NAFTA on July 1, 2020. With respect to E-1 and E-2 classification, the transition was seamless: the Department of State Treaty Country List continues to show January 1, 1994 as the effective date, reflecting that Canada's E-1/E-2 eligibility did not lapse during the transition. Companies registered as E-visa qualifying enterprises before July 1, 2020 retained their registrations.

The historical NAFTA Chapter 16 text remains relevant for interpreting the current USMCA Chapter 16 because the operative E-1/E-2 language was carried forward without material substantive change. Counsel briefing pre-2020 fact patterns or arguing the continuity of qualifying trade should cite both NAFTA Chapter 16 Section B and USMCA Chapter 16 Annex 16-A Section B.

## USMCA Labor-Dispute Carve-Out and the Regulations

The USMCA labor-dispute exception (USMCA Article 16.4(2), reproduced above) is implemented in U.S. regulations at:

- 22 CFR 41.51(a)(13) - E-1 (Treaty Trader): denial where the alien's temporary entry would adversely affect the settlement of a labor dispute or the employment of a person involved in such a dispute.
- 22 CFR 41.51(b)(14) - E-2 (Treaty Investor): same labor-dispute carve-out as applied to E-2.

These provisions, already addressed in the 02-Regulations file in this stack, are the U.S. implementation of USMCA Article 16.4(2)(a) and (b). For Canadian applicants the labor-dispute carve-out is the most concrete USMCA-derived ground of ineligibility specific to NAFTA/USMCA E adjudication, as distinct from the standard E criteria applied uniformly across treaty countries.
