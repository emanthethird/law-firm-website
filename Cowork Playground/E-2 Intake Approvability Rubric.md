# E-2 Intake Approvability Rubric (Canada)

A working scoring instrument for screening Canadian E-2 treaty investor prospects against the controlling authorities in the E-2 Authority Stack. Use at first intake to grade each statutory/regulatory element, surface red flags, and generate a punch list of strengthening actions before the file is built.

**Authority basis:** INA 101(a)(15)(E)(ii), 214(b); 22 CFR 41.51(b); 8 CFR 214.2(e); 9 FAM 402.9-4 through 402.9-6; *Matter of Walsh and Pollard*, 20 I&N Dec. 60 (BIA 1988); *Matter of Udagawa*, 14 I&N Dec. 578 (Reg. Comm'r 1974); *Matter of Ognibene*, 18 I&N Dec. 425 (Reg. Comm'r 1983). All citations cross-reference files in the `E-2 Authority Stack (Canada)` library.

**Scoring per element:** Green = clearly meets the test on intake facts. Yellow = arguable, fixable, or under-documented. Red = facially fails or triggers a bar. The case overall is **not approvable** until every element is at least Yellow with a credible path to Green by filing. Any Red element should be resolved or affirmatively waived before the package is submitted to Toronto.

---

## Quick-look intake screen (kill-switch questions)

Ask these first. A "no" or wrong answer on any of these usually ends the conversation or radically reshapes strategy.

1. Is the principal applicant a **Canadian citizen** (not just a PR)? *(Permanent residents of Canada do not qualify.)*
2. If applying through a business: is the business **at least 50% owned by Canadian citizens** (traced through to ultimate individuals)? Any owners who are **U.S. LPRs**? *(LPR shares don't count toward treaty nationality.)*
3. Are the **invested funds already committed** (not just earmarked or pledged)? Or in escrow contingent on visa issuance?
4. Were the **funds lawfully sourced** and can the client document the source?
5. Is the enterprise **for-profit** and **real/active** (operating or about to be operational), not just a holding entity or undeveloped real estate?
6. Has the applicant **ever overstayed** a prior U.S. admission, been **refused a visa**, been **denied entry**, had **criminal contact** (including DUI), or made any **misrepresentation** on a prior U.S. filing?
7. Is the applicant **physically in the U.S. now**? If so, status? *(Affects COS vs. consular routing; bars apply if VWP entry or 214(b) presumption issues exist.)*
8. **Dual nationality?** If the client may want to invoke a non-Canadian treaty nationality, flag *Ognibene* immediately.
9. Does the applicant intend to **depart at the end of E-2**, or is there a pending immigrant petition / clear adjustment plan? *(E permits a form of dual intent but a pending IV needs handling.)*

## Element 1 — Treaty Nationality

**Authority:** INA 101(a)(15)(E); 22 CFR 41.51(b)(6); 8 CFR 214.2(e)(7); 9 FAM 402.9-4(B); *Matter of Ognibene*.

**The test:** The individual investor (or, if the investor is a business, the ultimate individual owners holding ≥50%) must be a national of the treaty country. For Canada, treaty status flows from NAFTA continued under USMCA Chapter 16. Canadian PRs do not qualify. U.S. LPRs cannot count toward the 50% Canadian-ownership figure and cannot themselves be the principal.

**Green:** Single Canadian-citizen investor with Canadian passport, no other nationalities, no U.S. LPR status. Or: corporation with ≥50% individual Canadian-citizen ownership cleanly documented through share certificates and corporate matrix.

**Yellow:** Multi-tier ownership (Canco owns U.S. enterprise via holding company chain), dual-national investor who has not yet chosen the operative nationality, ownership documentation incomplete, recent share transfers, 50/50 JV where develop-and-direct is by "negative control."

**Red:** Investor is a Canadian PR but not citizen; investor is a U.S. LPR; <50% Canadian ownership traced to ultimate individuals; ownership is held through a trust or layered entity that cannot be traced; *Ognibene* problem — applicant in the U.S. on Canadian passport but file is built around a different treaty nationality.

**How to strengthen:**
- Build a **corporate matrix** that traces ownership through every entity layer to named individuals and their nationalities; attach to share certificates and stock ledger.
- For dual nationals, **commit to one nationality in writing** for E purposes (9 FAM 402.9-4(B)(d)) and hold consistent across DS-160, DS-156E, and counsel cover letter.
- For *Ognibene*-affected clients, route via **consular processing on the chosen passport**, not COS.
- If U.S. LPR shares exist, **restructure to push them below 50%** or buy them out before filing.
- For trust ownership, get **trustee certifications** identifying beneficiaries by nationality.

## Element 2 — Bona Fide Enterprise (Real & Active)

**Authority:** 22 CFR 41.51(b)(8); 8 CFR 214.2(e)(13); 9 FAM 402.9-6(C).

**The test:** Real, active, operating commercial or entrepreneurial undertaking producing goods or services for profit, meeting state/local legal requirements to do business. Not a paper company, not undeveloped land, not passive stock holdings, not a non-profit.

**Green:** Operating business with revenue history (or new-business plan with leased premises, inventory, employees, licenses, and a near-term launch); clean state formation; all required licenses/permits in place; commercial bank account in use; insurance bound; suppliers/customers identified.

**Yellow:** Pre-launch with leased space but no employees yet; permits in process; LLC/Inc formed but EIN not obtained; revenue model unproven; sector-specific licensing pending.

**Red:** Investment is undeveloped real estate held for appreciation; investment is passive securities; business is non-profit; entity exists only on paper with no commercial activity in motion; investor is the only "employee" in a service business with no clients or pipeline.

**How to strengthen:**
- Pull **all formation, licensing, and tax IDs** (state Articles, EIN letter, state and local business license, sales tax permit, sector permits like ABC, food, contractor's license).
- Show **physical signs of operation**: signed commercial lease, utilities in business name, business insurance, commercial bank statements with operating transactions, supplier invoices, point-of-sale or invoicing system in use.
- For new businesses, attach a **detailed 5-year business plan** with revenue and headcount projections, market research from Chamber/trade association, and timeline to launch.
- Bring at least one **non-investor employee on payroll** before filing where feasible (also helps marginality).
- Avoid framing the business as "real estate investment" — even if real estate-adjacent, position it as an active operating company (property management, short-term rental ops, brokerage, development).

## Element 3 — Investment (Source, Possession, Control, At-Risk)

**Authority:** 22 CFR 41.51(b)(7); 8 CFR 214.2(e)(12); 9 FAM 402.9-6(B).

**The test:** The investor's own capital must be (a) lawfully sourced, (b) in the investor's possession and control before commitment, (c) at risk in the commercial sense (subject to partial or total loss), and (d) either unsecured personal capital or capital secured by **personal** (not business) assets. Mortgage debt or commercial loans **collateralized by the business itself do not count**.

**Green:** Personal savings, lawful gift, inheritance, sale of personal real property, or unsecured personal-signature loan, with a clean paper trail from origin to commitment account. Investor's name on all source documents. Capital deployed (or in escrow) before interview.

**Yellow:** Mixed source (some personal, some loan); gift from family without gift letter; older sales/inheritance with gaps in paper trail; foreign-currency moves with no FX records; personal loan secured partly by personal and partly by business assets.

**Red:** Funds are loan proceeds **secured by the U.S. enterprise itself** (or assets to be acquired); funds source is criminal/illicit; funds belong to a third party who is not the applicant (employer, undisclosed partner, family business not owned by applicant); investor does not have demonstrable possession/control before commitment.

**How to strengthen:**
- **Source-of-funds memo** prepared by counsel with exhibits: bank statements showing accumulation, sale-of-asset closings, gift deeds with notarized letters, inheritance probate records, tax returns showing income matched to savings.
- **Wire trail**: every dollar from source → personal account → investment account / escrow / business account. No cash. No intermediate accounts in non-investor names.
- For personal loans, attach **promissory note and personal collateral documentation** (e.g., home appraisal, second-mortgage filing). Confirm in writing the loan is **not secured by the U.S. business or its assets**.
- For inherited funds, attach **probate, will, estate distribution records**.
- For gift funds, attach **donor declaration, donor's source of funds, and bank trail from donor**.
- If funds are still in Canada, **move now** to an escrow or U.S. operating account so the funds are demonstrably committed and at risk by interview.

## Element 4 — Irrevocable Commitment ("Has invested or actively in the process of investing")

**Authority:** 22 CFR 41.51(b)(7); 8 CFR 214.2(e)(12); 9 FAM 402.9-6(B)(d)-(e).

**The test:** Funds must be committed, not merely earmarked. Mere intent, uncommitted bank balance, or "looking for a location" does not satisfy. Escrow conditioned on visa issuance is acceptable. Applicant must be **close to the start of actual operations**.

**Green:** Funds already deployed: business purchased, lease signed, equipment paid, inventory in place, payroll started, or — at minimum — funds placed in attorney/title-company escrow with binding release instructions tied to E-2 issuance.

**Yellow:** Funds sitting in a personal/business account labeled "for the U.S. business" but no binding obligations yet; signed LOI with refundable deposit; lease negotiated but not signed.

**Red:** Money in the bank but no contracts, no lease, no entity, no operational steps; "scouting for a location"; "I'll commit when I get the visa" with no escrow; refundable deposits that can be unilaterally withdrawn.

**How to strengthen:**
- Move to **escrow** with conditional release tied to visa issuance — DOS expressly endorses this mechanism (9 FAM 402.9-6(B)(d)).
- Convert refundable LOIs and deposits into **binding, non-refundable** commitments where commercially possible.
- Sign and date the **commercial lease**, **purchase agreement**, **franchise agreement**, **supplier contracts**, **payroll service contract** — paper the commitment.
- Show evidence the business is at or near operational launch: **soft-opening photos, opening-day flyers, inventory on shelves, employees hired, soft-launched website with live SKUs**.

## Element 5 — Substantial Amount of Capital (Proportionality)

**Authority:** 22 CFR 41.51(b)(9); 8 CFR 214.2(e)(14); 9 FAM 402.9-6(D); *Walsh and Pollard*.

**The test:** No bright-line minimum. Inverted sliding scale: the lower the cost of the enterprise, the higher the percentage of investment required (often near 100% for low-cost service businesses; lower percentages tolerated for capital-intensive ventures). The investment must be (i) substantial in proportion to the total cost of purchasing or creating the enterprise, (ii) sufficient to show financial commitment, and (iii) of a magnitude to support successful development and direction.

**Green:** Service business or low-cost enterprise where investor has put in **100% or near-100%** of total cost; or capital-intensive enterprise where the investor's share, though a lower percentage, is in the high six- to seven-figures and clearly material. Total cost is documented with appraisals, market surveys, or arms-length sale price.

**Yellow:** Total cost figure is vague or self-serving; investor has put in 40–70% of a low-cost service business (likely insufficient); franchise where the franchise fee is small but build-out costs are large and partly financed by business-secured loan.

**Red:** Investor has put in a small fraction of a low-cost enterprise — e.g., $30K into a $300K consulting business; investment figure pumped by including non-qualifying loans, leases at multi-year totals, or speculative goodwill; total cost figure invented without market support.

**How to strengthen:**
- Build the **proportionality table** in the cover letter: total enterprise cost (with supporting documentation: appraisal, market survey, comparable-business pricing) vs. qualifying investment (itemized).
- For new enterprises, get a **CPA-signed startup-cost projection** that uses trade-association statistics, Chamber of Commerce estimates, and vendor quotes.
- For purchase of an existing business, attach the **fair-market-value appraisal**, the executed APA, and closing statements.
- Avoid inflating the cost denominator (it just raises the substantiality bar).
- For low-cost service businesses, aim for **100% qualifying capital deployed pre-interview**.
- Where the cost is genuinely low because of the nature of the business, lean on *Walsh and Pollard* in the brief: substantiality is proportional to the nature of the business.

### Practical capital floors (intake guidance)

**The authorities set no minimum.** 9 FAM 402.9-6(D)(b) is explicit: *"No set dollar figure constitutes a minimum amount of investment to be considered 'substantial' for E-2 visa purposes."* The regulations agree (22 CFR 41.51(b)(9); 8 CFR 214.2(e)(14)). *Walsh and Pollard* confirms that small investments can qualify when proportionate to the nature of the business.

But the practical floor in adjudication is set by two real constraints that interact:

- **Proportionality (Element 5):** inverted sliding scale. The FAM's illustrative example is a $100,000 startup where **100% qualifying investment would normally qualify** (9 FAM 402.9-6(D)(c)). That's the closest thing to a benchmark in the authority stack. Below that, the math still works but adjudicator scrutiny rises.
- **Marginality (Element 6):** a low absolute number frequently fails not because it isn't "substantial in proportion," but because the resulting enterprise cannot generate more than minimum family income or significant economic contribution within 5 years (9 FAM 402.9-6(E)).

**Working tiers for Canadian E-2 intake** (practitioner reality, not in the FAM):

| Capital deployed | Risk profile | When workable |
|------------------|--------------|---------------|
| **Under ~$50K** | Very high refusal risk | Theoretically allowed by *Walsh and Pollard* for asset-light service businesses, but requires an unusually clean proportionality story plus credible jobs/economic-contribution narrative. Most counsel won't file under this number absent extraordinary facts. |
| **$50K–$100K** | "Small E-2" zone — case-specific | Workable for consulting, professional services, e-commerce, IT services if (a) investment is genuinely ~100% of total cost, (b) at least one non-investor employee is on payroll or imminent, and (c) the business plan defeats marginality on the economic-contribution prong. |
| **$100K–$200K** | Typical Canadian small-business E-2 sweet spot | Covers most franchise, restaurant, retail, and trades businesses with clean documentation. Substantiality rarely a refusal driver in this range if proportional. |
| **$200K+** | Comfortable | Substantiality essentially a non-issue with proper documentation; focus shifts to marginality, source-of-funds, and develop-and-direct. |

**Intake working floor:** plan on **≥ $100K in qualifying capital deployed pre-interview** as a default. Below that, the case must have specific structural advantages — a project-oriented service offering, a clean *Walsh and Pollard* fact pattern, or substantial post-launch hiring already in motion — before filing.

**Caveat:** these are working numbers based on the FAM's $100K example and observed adjudication patterns. They are not legal thresholds. A $40K investment in the right business can be approved; a $300K investment in the wrong business can be refused.

## Element 6 — Not Marginal

**Authority:** 22 CFR 41.51(b)(10); 8 CFR 214.2(e)(15); 9 FAM 402.9-6(E).

**The test:** Two ways to defeat marginality: (a) present capacity to generate income materially exceeding minimum living needs for the investor's family, OR (b) significant economic contribution — typically job creation. Future capacity must be **realizable within 5 years** of normal business activity.

**Green:** Operating business with U.S. tax returns showing net income well above family-of-four poverty thresholds, plus W-2 employees; or new business with a 5-year financial projection (revenue, COGS, payroll, net income), supported by a business plan, showing rising income and **multiple non-investor U.S. hires** by year 2–3.

**Yellow:** Solo consultancy with no employees and modest projections; business plan with optimistic numbers but thin market analysis; revenue projected but headcount stays at one.

**Red:** Five-year plan never crosses the family-living threshold; no employees and no plan to hire; business model is fundamentally a self-employment vehicle.

**How to strengthen:**
- Build **3- to 5-year pro forma financials** with revenue, COGS, OpEx, headcount, and net income line items, plus footnoted assumptions tied to industry comps (Bureau of Labor Statistics, IBISWorld, trade-association data).
- Show **hires already on payroll** (W-2 employees, not 1099s where possible) before filing.
- Quantify the **economic contribution**: jobs created, supplier spending in U.S. economy, tax revenue, U.S. exports if applicable.
- For solo professional models, restructure the operating plan around hiring a manager/staff in year 1.
- Avoid framing the business as one designed to support the investor and family — the regulation expressly excludes that as "marginal."

## Element 7 — Develop and Direct

**Authority:** INA 101(a)(15)(E)(ii); 22 CFR 41.51(b)(11); 8 CFR 214.2(e)(16); 9 FAM 402.9-6(F).

**The test:** Investor controls the enterprise — by ≥50% ownership, by operational managerial control, by "negative control" in a 50/50 two-party JV, or by other means. The applicant must come **solely** to develop and direct (read as "primarily" per FAM).

**Green:** Single investor owns 100% (or ≥50% with documented executive role). Title and duties show genuine policy/direction-setting authority. Operating agreement / bylaws reserve decision-making to the investor.

**Yellow:** Investor at exactly 50% with another individual where governance terms are unclear; three-party equal partnership (DOS does not extend negative-control theory beyond two parties); investor has title but corporate documents vest control elsewhere.

**Red:** Investor <50% with no managerial control; investor is a passive financial backer with another party running operations; corporate documents reserve all material decisions to a non-treaty-national board.

**How to strengthen:**
- For 50/50 JVs, **document equal management rights** in the operating agreement so the negative-control theory applies (only works with two parties).
- Where ownership is <50% but the case rests on managerial control, attach **executed operating agreement / bylaws / employment agreement** vesting hire/fire, capital deployment, banking, contract signature, and strategy authority in the applicant.
- Build an **org chart** that visibly puts the applicant at the top with direct reports.
- Title alone is insufficient — show **discretionary decisions actually being made** (board minutes, signed contracts, hiring decisions).

## Element 8 — Intent to Depart (214(b))

**Authority:** INA 214(b); 22 CFR 41.51(b)(2)(iii); 8 CFR 214.2(e)(5); 9 FAM 402.9-4(C).

**The test:** Unequivocal intent to depart upon termination of E-2 status. E uniquely permits the applicant to sell the Canadian residence and move household effects. Pending immigrant petition or labor cert is **not by itself** a ground for refusal of E (8 CFR 214.2(e)(5)), but applicant must still satisfy the officer they will depart at end of authorized stay.

**Green:** Clear written statement of intent; no pending immigrant petition; explicit acknowledgement that E status is temporary; family ties or business interests in Canada that remain.

**Yellow:** Pending I-140 or I-130 (workable but needs careful handling); prior status violations; applicant openly states a goal of permanent U.S. residence.

**Red:** Misrepresentation about intent on a prior application; recent denied AOS; pending asylum or other affirmative immigrant application.

**How to strengthen:**
- Cover letter includes a one-paragraph **statement of nonimmigrant intent** — applicant understands E is temporary and intends to depart at end of authorized stay.
- Do not over-claim Canadian ties (regulation does not require them) — but if they exist (Canadian business, real estate, family), mention briefly.
- If an I-140 / I-130 is pending, frame the application as a separate process from E and clarify that admission to E is sought regardless.
- Do not introduce U.S. real estate purchases for the family or U.S. school enrollments in a way that suggests permanent relocation as the goal of E-2 rather than business operation.

## Element 9 — Admissibility (212(a))

**Authority:** INA 212(a); 22 CFR Part 40; INA 212(d)(3)(A); 22 CFR 40.301.

**The test:** Applicant must be admissible under all of 212(a). Common E-2 trip-wires: CIMT and other criminal grounds (40.21–40.22), drug-related findings (40.23), prior misrepresentation (40.63, INA 212(a)(6)(C)), prior unlawful presence (40.92–40.93, INA 212(a)(9)(B)/(C)), prior removal, public charge.

**Green:** No prior U.S. immigration history beyond compliant B-1/B-2 entries; no arrests or convictions anywhere; no prior visa refusals; no prior I-94 overstays.

**Yellow:** Prior B-2 admissions with long stays (officer may probe for overstay or immigrant intent); one prior visa refusal under 214(b); old or minor criminal history that may not be a CIMT; DUI history (DOS automatic referral to panel physician possible).

**Red:** Prior overstay >180 days triggering 3- or 10-year bar; prior order of removal; misrepresentation finding on a prior application; CIMT or controlled substance conviction; prior immigration fraud.

**How to strengthen:**
- Run a **complete U.S. immigration history review** before filing: pull I-94 travel history, all prior visa stamps, all prior denials. Reconcile any anomalies.
- Obtain **certified copies of criminal records and dispositions** for any contact (including dropped charges) — provide to counsel for CIMT analysis.
- If any inadmissibility ground is triggered, evaluate **INA 212(d)(3)(A) nonimmigrant waiver** path; build the waiver record concurrently (rehabilitation, hardship, equities, reasons for travel).
- For DUI history: be prepared for **panel physician referral**; do not pretend it didn't happen.
- For prior 214(b) refusal: address the underlying reason head-on in the cover letter and assemble new evidence that overcomes it.

## Element 10 — Documentary & Procedural Readiness (Mission Canada / Toronto)

**Authority:** Mission Canada FAQ (centralized E-Visa intake); Toronto E-Visa unit checklist; 9 FAM 402.9-11.

**The test:** Functional, not statutory — but a file that fails the Toronto submission rules is returned and delays the case by months. New Cases and Renewals route exclusively through Toronto; all Mission Canada E-visa correspondence is via EVisaCanada@state.gov.

**Green:** Single PDF, ≤50 pages (excluding the exempt items: DS-160, DS-156E, G-28, appointment confirmation, civil documents, passport bio, dividers), ≤20 MB, tabbed with cover sheet and full index. Cover letter is concise (no lengthy regulatory recitations), states precisely how each E-2 element is met. DS-160 confirmation number matches the application number used to schedule the interview.

**Yellow:** Package within page limit but disorganized; cover letter is over-long with block-quoted FAM citations; documents in random order; passport bio page missing.

**Red:** Over 50 pages, over 20 MB, zip file, online folder, mismatched DS-160 number, missing DS-156E officer signature.

**How to strengthen:**
- Use the **Toronto tabbed-section structure** verbatim: I. Nationality, II. Ownership, IV. Investment, V. Marginality, VI. Real & Operating, VII. Executive/Essential.
- Cover letter ≤5 pages; one paragraph per element; cross-reference exhibit tabs; **omit** block-quoted regulation text.
- Pre-flight check: PDF size, page count, OCR'd, bookmarks, no zip.
- Confirm **DS-160 application number == appointment confirmation number** (per May 1, 2025 Mission Canada policy).
- For an **E-2 renewal**, use the abbreviated Toronto renewal packet (DS-160, DS-156E, MRV receipt, prior-visa expiry confirmation, two years of corporate tax returns first two pages, Schedule K, recent W-2 / 941). Do not over-document on a renewal.
- For company-registered employees, route through Calgary, Montreal, Ottawa, Vancouver, or Toronto — but **only** for already-registered companies. New company registrations always go to Toronto first.

## Special-case flags (don't miss)

- **Employee cases (not investor):** Use Element 1 for ownership (50% Canadian rule applies to the *employer*), then apply 9 FAM 402.9-7. For executive/supervisory employees, Walsh/Pollard's five-factor essential-skills test applies if the role is technical rather than managerial. *Udagawa* warns against using E-2 for skilled labor (cooks, technicians) absent genuine, firm-specific specialization.
- **Dual-national clients:** *Ognibene* — nationality at U.S. entry is fixed for that admission. If invoking a non-Canadian treaty country, route via consular processing on that passport; do not attempt COS in the U.S. on the wrong passport.
- **In-country COS:** If applicant is in the U.S. on B, F, H, L, etc., file Form I-129 with USCIS instead of consular. INA 248 bars COS for VWP entrants and 212(a)(9)(B)(i) inadmissibles. Status must be continuously maintained.
- **Real-estate-heavy enterprises:** Position as active operating company, not passive investment. Document the management activity that converts real estate ownership into an operating business.
- **Solo consultancies / one-person operations:** Marginality risk is the chief failure mode. Plan to hire before filing.
- **Franchise purchases:** Watch for the loan-collateralization trap (Element 3 Red) — most SBA-backed franchise loans are secured by business assets and don't count.
- **Funds still in Canada:** Move to U.S. escrow before interview. "Will fund on issuance" without escrow fails the "in process of investing" test.
- **Pending I-140 / I-130:** Disclose, frame as separate process, lean on 8 CFR 214.2(e)(5).

## What I need at intake (minimum)

- Applicant's passport(s), Canadian citizenship proof, any U.S. immigration history (I-94 print, prior visas, prior denials).
- Complete criminal/police history for every country lived in.
- Corporate documents: formation, ownership structure, share certificates, operating agreement / bylaws, organizational chart.
- Investment documents: source-of-funds paper trail, bank/wire records, escrow agreement (if applicable), purchase agreement / lease / franchise agreement.
- Business plan with 3- to 5-year financials and headcount plan (or, for existing businesses, last 2 years of U.S. corporate tax returns and W-2s).
- Licensing, permits, lease, insurance.
- Applicant's resume, education credentials, prior business / management experience.
- Family details (dependents who will accompany) with civil documents.
