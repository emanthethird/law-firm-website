# Viability Research: Pay-Per-Qualified-Intro Deal Sourcing

**Model under evaluation:** Charge buyers (PE firms, search funds, independent sponsors, strategic acquirers) a fixed dollar amount per owner conversation that meets the buyer's stated criteria — revenue range, industry, and willingness to discuss a sale.

**Research date:** August 2026

---

## 1. Verdict (TL;DR)

**Viable — as a wedge offering, with specific structural guardrails.** The model is regulatorily *safer* than the industry-standard success-fee model, prices well below the incumbent retainer commitment (lowering the barrier to trying a new vendor), and analogous pay-per-meeting pricing is already accepted in adjacent B2B markets. The three make-or-break issues are: (1) an airtight, contractual definition of "qualified," (2) working capital through the 60–90 day ramp before intros flow, and (3) resisting the volume-over-quality incentive the pricing model itself creates.

---

## 2. How the market prices this today (competitive landscape)

Nobody dominant sells deal origination strictly per-intro. The incumbent structures:

| Model | Typical pricing | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly retainer | $5k–$25k/mo (budget end: ~$2k/mo) | CAPTARGET (~$2k/mo, no success fee), Visbl, OutSearched |
| Success fee only | 1–5% of transaction value at close | Traditional buy-side advisors |
| Hybrid | $3k–$8k/mo + reduced success fee | Many buy-side firms |
| Marketplace | ~$4k–$8k/mo effective + success fee on close | Axial |
| Buy-side advisory (full service) | $15k–$50k/mo + 1–2% of EV | CT Acquisitions-tier advisors |

Total annual origination spend for a mid-market PE firm runs **$60k–$200k**. First closed deal from a sourcing service typically takes 9–18 months; qualified flow starts after a 60–90 day ramp.

**The gap:** buyers currently must commit $24k–$300k/year *before knowing whether the vendor can produce*. A per-intro price converts that vendor risk into a unit purchase. That is a genuine wedge — the same wedge "pay-per-appointment" vendors used against retainer-based SDR agencies.

**Proof the mechanism sells:** in B2B appointment setting, pay-per-qualified-meeting is an established category — 2025 benchmarks put a qualified B2B appointment at **$300–$600 typical, $550–$1,700 average per Clutch, up to $1,500+ for C-suite**. Every conversation in this model is with an owner/CEO about the most sensitive topic there is (selling the company), i.e., the top of that range or above it.

## 3. Pricing power: what is a qualified owner conversation worth?

Triangulating from three directions:

1. **Retainer-implied value.** A $10k/mo retainer that produces 5–10 genuinely qualified owner conversations/mo implies **$1,000–$2,000 per conversation** — and buyers already pay this, just bundled.
2. **Appointment-setting ceiling.** C-suite meetings clear $600–$1,500+; a sale-willing owner meeting a mandate is scarcer and higher-stakes than any SaaS demo.
3. **Downstream value.** Search-fund funnels run roughly 1,000–3,000 prospects → 200–400 first conversations → 50–100 qualified → 5–15 LOIs → 1 close. At ~50–100 qualified conversations per closed deal, even $2,000/intro implies ~$100–$200k of intro spend per acquisition — comparable to what buyers already spend annually on origination, but paid only on delivery.

**Recommended pricing:** **$1,000–$2,500 per qualified intro**, tiered by target EBITDA band (e.g., $1,000 for <$1M EBITDA targets, $2,500+ for $2–5M EBITDA), sold as **prepaid packs of 5–10** (solves working capital; see §6).

## 4. Unit economics (can it be delivered profitably?)

Direct-to-owner outreach benchmarks:

- Cold email reply rates: **1–5% baseline** for lower-middle-market acquisition outreach; 15–25% for top-quartile senders with tight targeting and signal-based personalization. Named-individual senders outperform generic firm addresses 3–5x.
- Direct mail to owner lists: ~10–11% response. LinkedIn/InMail: ~2x email, 10–25% on good targeting.
- Search-fund funnels show **~3–5% of contacted prospects become qualified conversations** (with persistent multi-channel effort); an email-led service should conservatively assume **1–2%**.

**Cost model per qualified intro (conservative, 1.5% contact→qualified rate):**

| Input | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Contacts needed per qualified intro | ~65–70 |
| List/data/enrichment | ~$1–2 per contact → $70–$140 |
| Sending infra + tooling (amortized) | ~$30–$60 |
| Human time (qualification calls, scheduling, SDR/analyst) | ~$150–$400 |
| **Fully loaded cost per qualified intro** | **~$300–$800** |

At $1,500 average price → **~45–75% gross margin**. Margin improves sharply with multi-channel outreach (mail + phone) and with reusable industry data assets across multiple buyer mandates. The model clears unit economics with room to spare; the constraint is *throughput and consistency*, not margin.

## 5. Regulatory analysis — surprisingly favorable

This is the strongest finding of the research.

- **Flat per-intro fees are the textbook-safe structure.** The SEC treats *transaction-based compensation* (payment contingent on a deal closing or scaled to deal size) as the key indicator of unregistered broker activity. A flat fee paid per introduction, owed regardless of whether any transaction follows, is exactly the structure securities counsel recommend for unregistered finders. The pay-per-qualified-intro model is *less* regulatorily exposed than the success-fee models incumbents use.
- **Belt-and-suspenders: the 2023 federal M&A broker exemption** (Exchange Act §15(b)(13), effective March 29, 2023) exempts M&A brokers from SEC registration for change-of-control transactions in privately held companies with **EBITDA <$25M or revenue <$250M** — which covers essentially the entire SMB/LMM target universe — provided the buyer will control and actively manage the business, and the broker avoids excluded activities (no capital raising, no handling funds, no shell companies).
- **Residual caution:** (a) state "blue sky" rules — a handful of states require M&A broker registration regardless of the federal exemption; (b) staying on the intro side of the line — the service should introduce and qualify, not negotiate terms or advise on the transaction; (c) pure asset sales generally aren't securities transactions at all, which further de-risks the smallest deals.

**Action item:** one-time securities counsel review (~$5–15k) of the service agreement and qualification script; cheap insurance for a durable structural advantage over success-fee competitors.

## 6. Demand-side evidence

- **Buyer population is growing and underserved at the small end.** 90+ first-time search funds raised in 2023 (all-time high, per Stanford's 2024 study), plus a large and growing independent-sponsor population and thousands of LMM PE firms with record dry powder. Search-tier buyers can't afford $15–50k/mo advisory; a per-intro price is sized exactly for them.
- **Supply of sellers is structurally expanding.** Over half of U.S. small businesses are owned by people 55+, most without succession plans; these companies rarely run broker-led processes — proprietary outreach is how they get found.
- **Buyers already state criteria in exactly this form.** PE mandates and search theses are expressed as industry + revenue/EBITDA range + geography, i.e., the product's qualification spec already matches how buyers think.

## 7. Principal risks and mitigations

1. **Qualification disputes (the #1 killer).** "Willing to discuss a sale" is subjective; owners take calls out of curiosity. *Mitigation:* contractual qualification checklist signed per mandate (revenue band evidenced, industry match, owner verbally confirms openness to an acquisition conversation on a recorded/summarized qualification call); buyer gets 5 business days to reject with reason; rejected intros replaced free, not refunded.
2. **Volume-over-quality moral hazard.** Per-unit pricing rewards the vendor for marginal intros. *Mitigation:* replacement guarantee, monthly volume caps per mandate, and publishing intro→LOI conversion stats — the model only compounds if buyers re-up packs.
3. **Exclusivity conflicts.** Introducing one owner to multiple paying buyers destroys the "proprietary deal flow" value proposition. *Mitigation:* each intro exclusive to one buyer for 60–90 days; this is a differentiator vs. Axial-style marketplaces and should be marketed as such.
4. **Ramp-period cash flow.** 60–90 days before flow starts, and per-intro means $0 revenue until delivery. *Mitigation:* prepaid intro packs (e.g., 5 intros at $1,500 = $7,500 upfront) plus a modest one-time mandate setup fee ($1,500–$3,000) covering list building — still an order of magnitude below incumbent annual commitments.
5. **Churn-by-success.** A search fund that closes stops buying. *Mitigation:* weight the client mix toward PE platforms doing serial add-on acquisitions (repeat mandates) rather than one-shot searchers.

## 8. Recommended validation path

1. **Sell before building:** pitch 15–20 searchers/independent sponsors (Searchfunder community is concentrated and reachable) on a 5-intro prepaid pack; 3–5 paid mandates validates willingness-to-pay.
2. **Pick one industry vertical** for the pilot so list-building and messaging assets are reused across mandates (this is where the margin lives).
3. **Instrument the funnel from day one:** contacts → replies → qualification calls → accepted intros → buyer meetings → IOIs/LOIs. The intro→LOI rate is the sales asset for the next 20 clients.
4. **Legal review** of the qualification definition and fee structure before the first paid mandate (see §5).
