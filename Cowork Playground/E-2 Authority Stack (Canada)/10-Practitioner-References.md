# E-2 Authority Stack - Practitioner References

Compiled 2026-05-12 for the Canada-scoped E-2 treaty investor authority library. This file is a reference index, not a substantive reproduction. Most of the materials below are paywalled, member-only, or proprietary; full text cannot be pulled and would be a copyright issue if it could. Each entry gives the citation, the access path, a substantive description of what the resource covers, and why E-2 practitioners cite it - particularly in Canadian-investor matters.

A note on access: AILA materials sit behind the AILA member portal at agora.aila.org. AILALink is a separate AILA-member search platform. Several secondary sources (Bender's Immigration Bulletin, LexisNexis-published Interpreter Releases) require a LexisNexis subscription or paid law-firm-library access. Free practitioner resources are noted as such.

## 1. AILA's Kurzban's Immigration Law Sourcebook - E-1 / E-2 Chapter

Citation. Ira J. Kurzban, Kurzban's Immigration Law Sourcebook (AILA Publications). Most recent edition as of 2026: the 19th edition (2024) remains the most recent print/PDF release, with a 20th edition anticipated; verify the current edition on the AILA Agora store before citing in any filing. The E chapter has been Chapter 4 in recent editions, covering E-1 treaty traders, E-2 treaty investors, and (in earlier editions) E-3 specialty occupation Australian nationals.

Access. AILA members at agora.aila.org. The Sourcebook is available in print and as a digital edition. Non-AILA members can purchase through AILA Publications. Many law-firm libraries hold both the current edition and several prior editions for historical reference.

What the E chapter typically covers. The Sourcebook E chapter is the standard practitioner's first stop. It walks through:

- Statutory basis (INA 101(a)(15)(E)) and regulatory framework (8 CFR 214.2(e) and 22 CFR 41.51).
- Treaty country and nationality, including the 50 percent ownership rule and how nationality is established for entity applicants.
- The investment requirement - what counts as an investment, irrevocable commitment, escrow arrangements, source and tracing of funds.
- Substantiality - the proportionality test and how it has been applied at the consular and AAO levels.
- The bona fide enterprise requirement and the no-marginality rule, including five-year projections and the significant-economic-contribution alternative.
- Develop-and-direct and the employee categories (executive/supervisory, essential skills).
- Procedure - I-129 petition practice versus DS-160 consular practice, post-by-post variation, registration requirements.
- Period of stay, extensions, change of status, derivative spouse and child status.
- Recurring issues: dual intent, prior status violations, the interaction between E-2 and EB-5, the interaction between E-2 and L-1.

Why E-2 practitioners cite it. Kurzban is the closest thing the field has to a single authoritative treatise on the practitioner-facing rules. The E chapter cross-references the FAM, the regulations, the AAO non-precedent decisions, and post-specific practice in a way that the agency materials alone do not. For a Canadian E-2 matter, the Kurzban E chapter is the orientation read; for actual filing, practitioners go to the FAM and the USCIS Policy Manual directly (files 03 and 04 in this stack).

## 2. AILA Practice Pointers and Practice Advisories - E Filings

Citation. AILA Practice Pointers and Practice Advisories on E-1 / E-2 filings, posted as PDF documents at agora.aila.org/practice-advisories. Each is identified by an AILA Doc. No. (the format is YYNNNNNN, e.g., AILA Doc. No. 24010301).

Access. AILA members through the practice-advisory section of Agora; the index is searchable by topic and by date.

What they cover. AILA practice pointers on E filings tend to be tactical and post-specific. Common recurring topics include:

- E-2 company registration procedures at specific consular posts, particularly when posts change registration document checklists or evidentiary expectations.
- Source-of-funds documentation strategies for investors whose capital flows through multiple personal and corporate accounts.
- Marginality rebuttal strategies, including how to structure a five-year projection and what evidentiary support to gather.
- E dependent spouse employment authorization after the 2021 USCIS policy update treating E-2 dependent spouses as employment-authorized incident to status (no separate EAD required).
- E-2 essential-skills employee filings - what evidentiary record stands up.
- E-2 change-of-status practice on I-129 versus consular processing, with the comparative cost-benefit analysis.

Why E-2 practitioners cite it. These materials reflect what AILA members are seeing in actual filings - post-by-post quirks, RFE patterns, examiner trends - and are usually more current than the printed treatises. For Canadian E-2 work, search the practice-advisory index for "Toronto," "Montreal," "Calgary," "Vancouver," "EVisaCanada," and "Canada E-2."

## 3. AILA / DOS Liaison Q&As

Citation. AILA-DOS Liaison Committee Meeting Notes / Q&As. Published annually (sometimes twice annually) following the AILA-DOS liaison meeting. Each is posted as a PDF with an AILA Doc. No.

Access. AILA members through Agora. Search by year and by "DOS liaison."

What they cover. These are formal Q&As submitted by the AILA-DOS liaison committee to the Department of State (typically the Visa Office and Office of Visa Services within the Bureau of Consular Affairs), with DOS's written responses. The questions are submitted by practitioners and cover interpretive issues that have come up in actual cases. Recurring E-2 topics include:

- DOS position on marginality and the five-year projection (what level of detail DOS expects).
- DOS position on funds in escrow conditioned on visa issuance.
- DOS position on the develop-and-direct requirement for minority investors with operational control through corporate device.
- DOS position on the treatment of "real and operating" enterprise in early-stage and pre-revenue contexts.
- DOS position on essential-skills employees - what training, what duration, what evidence.
- DOS position on E-2 holders' use during transition periods (e.g., status maintenance during U.S.-side change-of-employer scenarios).

Why E-2 practitioners cite them. The DOS responses in the liaison Q&As are the closest publicly available source for current DOS interpretive positions on E-2 issues - more current than the FAM updates and more authoritative than the practitioner blog discussions. They are particularly valuable for documenting "this is the agency's stated position" in a contested case. Cite as "AILA-DOS Liaison Q&A [Date], AILA Doc. No. [number]."

## 4. AILA / USCIS Liaison Q&As (cross-reference for change-of-status and extension matters)

Citation. AILA-USCIS Liaison Committee Meeting Notes / Q&As. Published periodically. Posted as PDF with AILA Doc. Nos.

Access. AILA members through Agora.

What they cover. The USCIS counterpart to the DOS Q&As. Less directly on point for E-2 because USCIS handles E-2 only on I-129 change-of-status and extension-of-stay petitions (not initial visa issuance), but valuable for:

- I-129 E classification supplement issues (Question 16, dependent employment authorization, etc.).
- Premium processing eligibility and timing for E filings.
- Status-maintenance and timely-filing rules.
- USCIS RFE patterns on E-2 substantiality and marginality.
- Interaction of E status with other USCIS-adjudicated benefits (adjustment of status, H-1B portability, etc.).

Why E-2 practitioners cite it. Where the matter is a U.S.-side I-129 (e.g., change of status from B-1/B-2 or F-1 to E-2, or an extension filed without leaving the U.S.), the USCIS liaison Q&As are the right interpretive source, not the DOS Q&As.

## 5. DOS Press Guidance, CA/VO Cables, and FAQ Memoranda

Citation. Department of State Bureau of Consular Affairs press guidance, internal Visa Office (VO) cables, and FAQ memoranda. Generally not published on travel.state.gov in full text; some are summarized or excerpted in AILA's "Featured Issues" section and AILA practice advisories.

Access. Mixed. Some appear on travel.state.gov; some are released through FOIA; some are reported in AILA materials when AILA receives them. Bender's Immigration Bulletin and Interpreter Releases (see entries below) sometimes publish the text of leaked or FOIA-released cables.

What they cover. Operational guidance to consular posts that does not always make it into the FAM in the same form. Recurring useful items:

- DOS press guidance on E-2 country-list changes (e.g., addition of New Zealand, sunset and reinstatement of Israel).
- VO cables on processing priorities, COVID-era visa-services modifications, and the gradual return to normal interview volumes (now historical but still cited).
- Internal FAQ memoranda on the treatment of post-treaty acquisitions of nationality (for E-2, the five-year-residency rule for naturalized nationals of the treaty country).
- DOS guidance on third-country processing (cited in the rare cases where a Canadian E-2 applicant cannot interview in Canada).

Why E-2 practitioners cite them. Where the FAM is silent or ambiguous and there is no AAO non-precedent on point, a contemporaneous DOS press release or cable is sometimes the only available authority for a specific consular practice. For Canadian E-2 work, the most useful items are post-specific announcements about EVisaCanada@state.gov processing changes and company-registration document checklists, which AILA's Toronto / Montreal / Calgary / Vancouver chapters typically circulate when they appear.

## 6. AILALink - AILA Members' Search Platform

Citation. AILALink, AILA's full-text search platform integrating the BIA / AAO decisions, the FAM, the USCIS Policy Manual, the INA and regulations, AILA Practice Advisories, AILA Liaison Q&As, the Kurzban Sourcebook, and selected secondary materials.

Access. AILA members through ailalink.aila.org (separate login from Agora in some configurations).

What it covers. Search across the entire AILA-aggregated corpus from a single query. The integrated nature is the value-add - a single search for "E-2 marginality five-year projection" returns the relevant FAM provision, the Kurzban discussion, the AAO non-precedents, and the practice-pointer guidance in one result set.

Why E-2 practitioners cite it. AILALink is a research tool rather than a citation source - the cites are to the underlying materials, not to AILALink itself. But it is the most efficient way to find those underlying materials, particularly the AAO non-precedent decisions on E-2 matters (which are not easily searchable through USCIS's own AAO non-precedent search interface).

## 7. Bender's Immigration Bulletin

Citation. Bender's Immigration Bulletin (LexisNexis / Matthew Bender). Semi-monthly periodical with reported BIA / AAO decisions, federal court immigration decisions, agency guidance, and practitioner articles.

Access. LexisNexis subscription (most law-firm libraries with immigration practices subscribe). Selected articles are publicly available through the LexisNexis Immigration Law Community blog.

What it covers. Long-form practitioner articles on immigration-law developments, plus full-text of significant new decisions. E-2 coverage tends to appear when there is a notable federal court decision touching E classification or a substantive change in the FAM, USCIS Policy Manual, or treaty-country list.

Why E-2 practitioners cite it. Useful for tracking the federal court case law that supplements the AAO non-precedent universe, and for picking up significant FAM changes when AILA practice advisories have not yet appeared. Bender's articles are also sometimes cited as secondary authority in particularly technical motions before the AAO.

## 8. Interpreter Releases

Citation. Interpreter Releases (Thomson Reuters / formerly West / formerly Federal Publications). Weekly immigration law reporter, published since 1924. Edited by a rotating panel of senior immigration practitioners and academics.

Access. Westlaw subscription. Many law-firm libraries also hold the print run.

What it covers. The longest-running periodical in U.S. immigration law. Weekly coverage of agency actions, regulatory developments, federal court decisions, and practitioner-oriented analysis. E-2 coverage appears in the same situations as Bender's, but with longer historical depth - Interpreter Releases is often the only source for analysis of an older FAM provision or a Reagan-era DOS cable that remains operative.

Why E-2 practitioners cite it. For historical depth on E classification (the E-2 framework has been substantially unchanged since the 1990 regulations) and for tracking the trajectory of DOS interpretive positions over multi-decade timeframes.

## 9. Federation of American Scientists FAM Mirror and Other Historical FAM Archives

Citation. Federation of American Scientists, Foreign Affairs Manual mirror, https://irp.fas.org/fam/ and predecessor URLs (the FAS index page sometimes captures older or removed FAM volumes).

Access. Free, public.

What it covers. FAS periodically scrapes and archives Department of State Foreign Affairs Manual volumes, including 9 FAM 402.9 and adjacent provisions. Where DOS removes or substantively revises a FAM provision and the practitioner needs to point to the prior text, FAS is often the cleanest free archive. Cross-reference: archive.org's Wayback Machine for fam.state.gov captures provides a similar historical view; archive.today does too.

Why E-2 practitioners cite it. Pre-revision FAM text is sometimes load-bearing - for example, when a client filed in reliance on a prior FAM standard and a subsequent change shifts the analysis. The FAS mirror provides a citable archived URL when the live FAM page no longer reflects the version the client relied on. The practitioner still cites 9 FAM 402.9 with the date of the version in question; FAS is the source for the text, not the citation.

## 10. Immigration Practitioner Bulletins and Blogs - VisaSpot, Murthy, Cyrus Mehta, NAFSA, Stuart Anderson at Forbes

Citation. Various.

- Murthy Law Firm bulletin, https://www.murthy.com/news/news.html. Weekly practitioner bulletin with free access. Strong on USCIS-side practice issues.
- Cyrus D. Mehta & Partners blog, https://blog.cyrusmehta.com. Free. Frequent thoughtful analysis of E, L, and EB-5 issues at the intersection.
- VisaSpot and other commercial visa-tracking platforms - useful for wait-time data and post-by-post trends.
- NAFSA: Association of International Educators, https://www.nafsa.org. Primarily F/J/student focused; occasionally relevant for E-2 family members on F-1.
- Stuart Anderson at National Foundation for American Policy and at Forbes - useful for policy-context citations supporting economic-contribution and marginality-rebuttal narratives.
- Greg Siskind (Siskind Susser PC) blog, https://www.visalaw.com. Free, frequent updates.

Access. All free.

What they cover. Current-developments commentary, RFE trend reporting, post-specific practice notes, and policy analysis. These are not authority - they are practitioner intelligence sources.

Why E-2 practitioners cite them. Generally not cited in filings. They are read for situational awareness and to identify which agency developments are worth running down to primary sources. The exception is the occasional thoughtful blog post by a recognized practitioner (Mehta, Siskind, Yale-Loehr) that summarizes a federal court decision or AAO non-precedent in a way that is useful to attach as a Tab in a complex filing - in that case, cite the underlying decision, not the blog.

## 11. Treatises Cross-Reference - Fragomen on Immigration; Yale-Loehr / Pendleton on Investment-Based Immigration

Citation.

- Austin T. Fragomen, Jr., et al., Immigration Law and Business (Thomson Reuters / Clark Boardman Callaghan, multi-volume looseleaf). Updated several times per year. The E classification chapter is the long-form business-immigration treatise treatment.
- Stephen Yale-Loehr and Carolyn Lee, Immigration Law and Procedure (LexisNexis / Matthew Bender, multi-volume looseleaf), particularly the chapters on nonimmigrant business categories. Also Yale-Loehr's earlier published work on investment-based immigration provides historical context on the E-2 / EB-5 boundary.

Access. LexisNexis or Thomson Reuters subscription (law-firm library or Westlaw / Lexis).

What they cover. Long-form treatise treatment of E classification, with full statutory and regulatory analysis, citation to federal court decisions, and citation to historical agency materials. More academic in tone than Kurzban; deeper than the Sourcebook on doctrinal questions.

Why E-2 practitioners cite them. Less common in routine filings; more common in motions, AAO briefs, and federal court litigation where doctrinal depth is needed. For a routine Canadian E-2 matter, Kurzban plus the FAM plus the USCIS Policy Manual is usually sufficient. For a contested matter or a motion before the AAO challenging an adverse decision, the Fragomen or Yale-Loehr treatise treatment is a useful supplemental authority.
