The Convergence Assay · v1.0

What are you
actually expert in?

You can't answer that by asking yourself how confident you feel — confidence is the least reliable signal of mastery. This instrument works differently. Genuine expertise leaves independent traces: things you know that aren't written anywhere, errors you catch before you can explain them, a hidden layer you see that others don't. We test your candidate domains against ten of those traces. The one domain where they converge is the answer.

Method · trace triangulation Runtime · ~4 min Output · one domain + certainty

01 Name your candidates

List the 2–4 domains where you might, plausibly, be an expert.

Don't overthink the wording, and don't be modest or grandiose — just name the territories. If you're stuck, the four prompts below tend to surface real candidates that self-assessment hides.

  • Asked What do people come to you about — unprompted, even when they could ask someone else?
  • Unpaid hours What have you poured uncounted hours into because you wanted to, not because you had to?
  • Irritation What do you see done wrong in a way that genuinely bothers you, when others don't even notice?
  • No prep What could you talk about for an hour, right now, with zero preparation?

Enter at least two candidates to begin.
